1. Abstract

ServiceHub is an Android app that connects customers with verified local service professionals such as plumbers, electricians, cleaners, AC technicians, carpenters and painters. Customers see clear fixed prices, book a professional in their area for a chosen date and time slot, and follow the job live on their phone. Providers receive requests, accept or reject them, start the job with a 4-digit PIN given by the customer, add any extra parts, collect payment and track their earnings. An admin approves providers, manages services, categories and coupons, handles complaints and can block users.

The app is written in Kotlin with Jetpack Compose and follows the MVVM architecture. All data is stored in Firebase Cloud Firestore, so two phones see every change live within about a second. Login uses Firebase Authentication. UPI payments are demonstrated with Razorpay in test mode (no real money), push notifications use Firebase Cloud Messaging, and a tiny free Vercel server performs the two actions that a phone app must never do itself (setting another user's password and sending pushes). Everything runs on free plans.

2. Introduction and problem statement

Finding a reliable local service provider is unorganised. People rely on word of mouth, cannot compare prices before calling, do not know whether the person is trustworthy, and cannot track a request after making it. Providers, on the other hand, have very little digital visibility and depend on a few regular customers.

Problem: there is no simple, trustworthy way for a household to find, book and track a nearby professional at a known price, and no simple way for a professional to get and manage jobs.

Solution: ServiceHub gives three kinds of users one shared app:

  • Customers browse services with fixed prices, choose an admin-verified professional in their area, book a slot, share a start PIN on arrival, pay by cash or UPI after the job, rate it, and get a free redo within 7 days if needed.
  • Providers manage requests, today's jobs, payments to collect and earnings in one place.
  • The admin keeps the platform trustworthy: approves providers, controls prices and offers, resolves complaints.

3. Objectives and scope

Objectives

  1. Build a real, working Android app (not a mock-up) that works on two real phones at the same time.
  2. Role-based app: one app, three homes (Customer, Provider, Admin), chosen automatically after login.
  3. Safe booking: no double booking, a start PIN, a clear status lifecycle and live tracking.
  4. Transparent money: fixed prices, a visible visit fee, itemised extras, a receipt, and earnings that count only money received.
  5. Use only free services (Firebase Spark plan, Vercel Hobby, Razorpay test mode).
  6. Keep the code simple and well commented so every part can be explained.

Scope (included)

Registration and login, provider approval, service catalogue and search, provider list with area filter, booking with slots, live status tracking, start PIN, cancel and reschedule, payment step (cash / UPI test), coupons, ratings and reviews, receipt, 7-day redo guarantee, chat, in-app and push notifications, complaints, admin dashboard and controls.

Out of scope (future)

Real-money payments, photo uploads, live GPS tracking, multiple languages, dark mode (see section 14).

A screen never talks to Firebase directly. It shows the state held by its ViewModel and sends user actions to it. The ViewModel checks the input and calls a repository. Repositories are the only code that reads and writes Firebase. Firestore listeners send every change back up as a Kotlin Flow, so all phones stay in sync. Payments go to Razorpay and come back to MainActivity; pushes arrive through PushService.

Every move runs in a Firestore transaction: it reads the current status and refuses any move that is not in this table. If two phones act at the same moment, only one change succeeds.

7. Modules and features

7.1 Customer

  • Home in sections: Needs your attention (pay, rate, provider did not answer), Your next booking with the start PIN, Book a service, Categories, Book again, Popular services.
  • Book a service: a short guided form – category, then service with price preview, then professional. Category tiles open the same form.
  • Provider list: starts in the customer's own area (falls back to all areas if nobody is there), sort by rating / experience / jobs, Available today, favourites, badges.
  • Booking form: date chips (provider's days off disabled), slots shown as Booked or On hold, saved addresses, book for someone else, problem note, Cash or UPI.
  • Review page: one summary card, price details, Offers for you (best first, one-tap Apply) and a coupon code box.
  • Booking detail: live status steps, Next step box, PIN card, call and chat, cancel, reschedule, switch payment method, Pay now (UPI test), receipt, rate, redo request with redo PIN, report a problem.
  • Profile: name, phone, area, address, saved addresses, change email, favourites, help, logout.

7.2 Provider

  • Waiting for approval screen until the admin approves.
  • Home in sections: Needs your attention (new requests, payments to collect, redo requests), Next job, Today's jobs, Earnings (Today, This week, To collect, weekly chart), Availability (switch and days off), Recent reviews.
  • Job detail: the action for the current step at the top – Accept / Reject, I'm on my way, PIN and Start, Complete with extra parts, Cash collected or automatic UPI payment received, redo with the customer's redo PIN – then call, maps, chat and booking details.
  • Requests grouped as New requests, Redo requests, Payments to collect, Upcoming; History of finished jobs.

7.3 Admin

  • Dashboard: needs attention (pending providers, complaints, redo and expired requests – each opens a filtered list), counts, earnings split between ServiceHub and providers, charts. Red badges on the tabs.
  • Approvals, Users (search, filter by role and category, block, reset link, change password), Bookings (filters and read-only detail with call buttons), Services, Categories, Coupons, Complaints, Load sample data.

8. Key workflows step by step

8.1 Booking and the 1-hour hold

  1. The customer confirms a booking. In one transaction the app checks the slot document and saves both the booking (REQUESTED, random 4-digit PIN) and the slot (held for 1 hour).
  2. Other customers now see that slot as On hold. The provider gets a notification and a push.
  3. If the provider accepts, the slot becomes Booked. If there is no answer within 1 hour, the hold ends: others may book the slot, and the customer is offered another provider or another time.

8.2 Doing the job

  1. The provider taps I'm on my way; the customer is told.
  2. On arrival the customer tells the PIN; the provider enters it and the job becomes IN_PROGRESS.
  3. The provider completes the job and may add extra parts; the new total is calculated and the job is added to the provider's totals in the same transaction.

8.3 Payment

  • Cash: only the provider can confirm it (Cash collected).
  • UPI: the customer taps Pay now; Razorpay (test mode) returns a payment ID; the app marks the booking PAID and the provider sees "UPI payment received" live with a push.
  • The customer can switch Cash ↔ UPI before paying. Earnings count only money actually received; unpaid jobs appear under To collect.

8.4 After the job

  • Rating: once per booking; the average is updated in a transaction: new average = (old average × old count + new rating) ÷ (old count + 1).
  • Redo within 7 days: the customer asks for a free redo and receives a redo PIN; the provider can mark it done only with that PIN.
  • Complaint: the customer reports a problem; the admin calls both sides and marks it resolved.

8.5 Notifications and push

Each event writes a notification document for the other person (live badge in the app). The app then asks the Vercel server to push it; the server checks that the sender belongs to that booking and sends it to the receiver's phone. Tapping the push opens that booking, or its chat for a message.

Example: race-proof booking (simplified from BookingRepository.createBooking)

db.runTransaction { t -> if (isTaken(t.get(slot), now)) throw "already booked"; t.set(slot, slotData); t.set(booking, data) }

Only one document can have the id providerId_date_slot, and the check and both writes happen together, so two customers can never get the same provider at the same time.

10. Security

  • Users edit only their own profile; they can never change their own role or blocked status. Admin is never selectable at registration.
  • Only the admin approves providers, blocks users and edits services, categories and coupons.
  • A booking is readable only by its customer, its provider and the admin. Each side may change only its own fields: a customer can never mark a job completed or cash collected, and can cancel or reschedule only while it is Requested or Accepted.
  • Provider totals can only go up by one completed job; ratings stay 1–5 and count up by one.
  • A discount is allowed only with an active coupon.
  • Chat messages and notifications are limited to the people on that booking.
  • Blocked users cannot write anything and are logged out at once.
  • Secret keys (Firebase service account, Razorpay) are never in the app or on GitHub; the service account lives only in Vercel's settings, the Razorpay test key in the local local.properties file.

14. Limitations and future scope

Limitations

  • Payments use Razorpay test mode, and the payment is recorded by the app, not verified on a server.
  • The provider's app downloads the booking including the PINs (they are hidden on screen only).
  • The coupon amount is calculated in the app; the rules check only that the coupon exists and is active.
  • The 1-hour "no answer" notice is created when the customer opens the app, not exactly at one hour.

Future scope

  • Server-side payment check (Razorpay order and signature), server-side price, coupon and PIN checks.
  • Timed alerts with a scheduled server; real (live-mode) payments after Razorpay KYC.
  • Photos of the problem and provider ID documents (needs Firebase Storage on the paid plan).
  • Maps and live location of the professional (needs a Maps billing account).
  • Phone OTP login, Hindi / Gujarati, dark mode, provider working hours and custom slots.

15. Conclusion

ServiceHub meets its goal of a real, working home-services app used on two phones at the same time. It covers the complete life of a booking – discovery, booking, live tracking, a secure start, completion, payment, rating, guarantee and support – for three roles in one app. Firestore transactions and security rules keep the data correct and protected, and the MVVM structure keeps each part small enough to explain. Building it taught the practical side of Android development: state-driven UI, real-time cloud data, concurrency, security, payments, push notifications and testing on real devices, all on free plans.
